\section{Acquisition Validity}
\label{sec:valid}

Knowledge acquired by a machine is only as good as its validation
\cite{okeefe_kbs_validation}. This section reports how the acquisition
component's output scores against professional gold, and what the errors
look like under a knowledge-quality taxonomy. Throughout, we read LLM
scores against the ceiling the data itself imposes---two professional
sources rarely agree exactly (Section~\ref{sec:data})---rather than against
exact match. Panel-validated gold and the measured inter-cataloger
agreement ceiling \TODO{E2} will upgrade the numbers reported here; the
conference-scale figures establish the method and the qualitative results.

\subsection{Multi-level agreement with professional gold}

The acquisition component proposes, per record, subject headings and a
Dewey number. We score the proposals against professional gold at three
levels, reflecting that different valid headings for the same concept are
legitimate \cite{okeefe_kbs_validation}: \emph{exact} (normalized string
match), \emph{semantic equivalence} (a different valid heading for the same
concept), and \emph{acceptable} (a correct broader heading). On
\CatRecords{} records with \CatGoldHeadings{} gold headings
\TODO{E1: scale}, exact agreement covers \CatExact\% of gold headings;
allowing semantic equivalence raises coverage to \CatSemantic\%, and
allowing a correct broader heading reaches \CatAcceptable\%. The choice of
scoring level is not cosmetic: exact match alone understates the
acquisition component by nearly a factor of two, exactly the gap the
HathiTrust cross-check (Section~\ref{sec:data}) predicts for professional
catalogers themselves. At the record level, \CatRecordsAnyExact\% of
records receive at least one exactly correct heading.

Classification acquisition is reported separately because the
representation is closed and hierarchical: exact Dewey agreement is
\CatDDCExact\% of gold-carrying records, rising to \CatDDCClass\% at the
3-digit class, with \CatDDCWrongBranch\% landing in the wrong top-level
class. The contrast between the two representations---\CatExact\% exact
subject-heading agreement versus \CatDDCClass\% three-digit class
accuracy---is the first appearance of the representation-dependence theme;
Section~\ref{sec:util} shows it is load-bearing for downstream utility.

\subsection{Error taxonomy and the unmatched residual}

Validation requires classifying what the acquisition component got wrong,
not just what it matched. Predicted headings that match no gold are
classified into a knowledge-quality taxonomy: \emph{over-specific},
\emph{under-specific}, \emph{authority-violating} (main heading absent from
the LC authority files, checked live against \texttt{id.loc.gov}), and
\emph{valid-but-unmatched} (valid LCSH that matches no gold heading for that
record). Fabrication---the failure mode that motivates authority
control---is rare: only \CatAuthorityViolations{} of \CatPredHeadings{}
predicted headings (\CatAuthorityRate\%) violate the authority files
\TODO{E3: confirm across models}.

The dominant residual is the \CatUnmatched{} valid-but-unmatched headings
(\TODO{E1: re-derive}). These are not errors by construction: the
HathiTrust cross-check shows two professional sources share an exact
heading for only a minority of books, so a predicted heading absent from one
source's gold may be a legitimate alternative rather than a mistake. We
decompose the residual using evidence internal to the knowledge base: of the
\CatUnmatched{} headings, \UnmGoldElsewhere{} are the \emph{gold heading of
another record} in the benchmark (a heading professionals demonstrably
assign), \UnmNearSem{} fall within one cosine band of the record's own gold
(near-paraphrases just under the semantic threshold), and \UnmFamily{}
share a main-heading family with another record's gold; the union carries
\UnmShare\% of the residual, leaving \UnmResidual{} as the honest upper
bound on genuine error \TODO{E2: adjudicate with the cataloger panel}. The
panel is what converts that bound into a measurement; until then, all
subject-heading scores in this paper are \emph{agreement with one
professional source}, not accuracy in an absolute sense.

\subsection{What drives acquisition quality}

Three levers could explain acquisition quality; we isolate what the current
data supports and what the journal extension must add.

\textbf{Representation type (measured).} The largest quality difference is
between representations, not within them: classification acquisition
(\CatDDCClass\% at the 3-digit class) is far more reliable than subject
acquisition (\CatExact\% exact) \TODO{E1: re-verify at scale}. The
mechanism is structural: Dewey is a closed, hierarchical vocabulary in
which the component can be approximately right at a coarser granularity;
LCSH is an open, pre-coordinated string vocabulary in which being exactly
right is harder and different valid answers abound. This is why
Section~\ref{sec:util} measures the two representations' downstream utility
separately.

\textbf{Memorization (measured, bounded).} These are canonical
public-domain works, so a large model may recall their real headings rather
than perform acquisition. Splitting records into popularity terciles by
download count, exact subject agreement runs
\PopLowExact{} / \PopMidExact{} / \PopHighExact{} from least to most
downloaded, and 3-digit Dewey accuracy
\PopLowDdc{} / \PopMidDdc{} / \PopHighDdc{}. There is a popularity effect
on exact heading agreement (\PopExactDelta{} from bottom to top tercile)
but it is non-monotonic, and the strong result---classification---is nearly
flat (\PopDdcDelta{}), as is the authority-violation rate
(\PopLowAuth\% vs.\ \PopHighAuth\%). Memorization does not appear to carry
the results, but the probe only bounds recall effects across well-known
works \TODO{E1: extend to a corpus with genuinely obscure material}.

\textbf{Conditioning (measured).} Giving the acquisition component a
head-of-text excerpt raises subject-heading quality materially while leaving
classification nearly unchanged. On the records scored in both conditions
($n=191$, the strictly paired set), any-level subject-heading coverage rises
from \EthreeLcshSparse{} to \EthreeLcshFull{} ($+\EthreeLcshDelta{}$)
while 3-digit classification rises only from \EthreeDdcSparse{} to
\EthreeDdcFull{} ($+\EthreeDdcDelta{}$); the difference of differences is
$+\EthreeDiff$ (95\% CI \EthreeDiffCI). The direction is the predicted
mechanism---content helps choose among open heading strings far more than it
helps place a work in a closed hierarchy---but the interval touches zero at
this sample size, so we report the interaction as suggestive rather than
resolved. Fabrication does not increase with content access (authority
violations: \EthreeViolSparse\% of predicted headings sparse vs.\
\EthreeViolFull\% with text).

\subsection{Summary for the knowledge-engineering claims}

Acquisition validity is a three-part statement. (i) Under a
multi-level rubric that respects legitimate inter-cataloger variation, the
LLM's subject-heading acquisition is materially better than exact-match
scoring suggests, and classification acquisition is strong at the class
level. (ii) Its errors are rarely fabrication; the dominant residual is
valid-but-unmatched knowledge whose legitimate-alternative share is bounded
by internal evidence and will be measured by the panel \TODO{E2}. (iii) The
quality profile differs sharply across representations, which is the
prediction Section~\ref{sec:util} tests downstream.
